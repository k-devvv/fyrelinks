# FyreLinkz launch and audience plan

Prepared September 12, 2026. The code and content are a foundation for readership; no traffic, rankings or ad revenue have been generated or guaranteed by this work.

## What to publish for

Focus the front page on people making AI video, learning ComfyUI and deciding whether local hardware fits their workload. Keep legacy developer articles accessible, but avoid expanding into unrelated topics merely to chase keywords. Google recommends original, useful content with clear sourcing and a consistent audience focus. [Google: helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

## Launch sequence

1. Review the new preview, sources and correction notice. Confirm the editorial mailbox works. Deploy the verified change to the existing Vercel project when approved.
2. Check the production homepage, three new news URLs, the calculator, all eleven old URLs and an invalid article/category combination. Confirm canonical tags, title text, article images and source links.
3. Submit the production sitemap in Search Console. Inspect the homepage and the AI-video, FLUX and workstation guides. Read actual indexing reasons; the supplied screenshot's short zero-click period does not establish an indexing failure.
4. Save a baseline of Search Console impressions, clicks, query and page data. Compare later periods of the same length. Keep paid and organic acquisition separate if paid promotion is introduced.
5. Before ad-network activation, use the real account's eligibility and integration requirements. Do not assume the old brief's RPM ranges or entry thresholds are current.

## Ready-to-adapt distribution copy

**Calculator introduction**

I built a small AI-video budget calculator for FyreLinkz. It converts clip pricing into cost per usable minute using your own accepted-footage rate. For example, a $0.50 five-second clip at 50% usable footage gives an illustrative $12 per usable minute. It does not assume any vendor's current prices. What costs do you track beyond generation—upscaling, rejected takes, audio or cleanup?

Use the calculator's deployed canonical URL once the new version is live. Post only where this contribution fits the community's current rules, and disclose ownership. The useful example should stand on its own even if nobody follows the link.

## Content structure to repeat

Headline describing the actual decision or event; short answer; publication date and separate event date; identifiable editorial byline; representative artwork; practical sections; inline attribution; primary-source list; limitations and correction path; related reading. Add original tests only when the raw evidence exists.

The redesign adapts common reading patterns—topic navigation, a prominent lead story, dated news and related guides—from [The Verge's AI section](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence), [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/) and [Creative Bloq's AI section](https://www.creativebloq.com/ai). It uses original layout details, text and artwork.

## SEO and revenue priorities

The implementation fixes duplicate title suffixes, category-insensitive article lookup, missing article images and absent visible citations. It retains existing slugs, exposes a working sitemap and RSS feed, and permits crawlers to load framework assets. These improvements make pages easier to browse and interpret; they do not compel indexing or rich results. [Google: article structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article)

Large article images and max-image-preview:large are included. Discover remains supplemental and unpredictable; no special schema guarantees placement. [Google: Discover](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover)

Prioritize repeat readers and useful page depth before adding more placements. Use labeled ads with stable dimensions. Avoid making the site harder to read to increase ad count. A basic revenue worksheet is page views ÷ 1,000 × actual observed page RPM; enter the rate from your own ad account rather than treating someone else's estimate as a forecast.
