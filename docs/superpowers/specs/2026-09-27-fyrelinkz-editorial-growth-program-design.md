# FyreLinkz Editorial Growth Program — Design

**Status:** Approved for staged implementation.

## Goal

Build FyreLinkz into a practical, evidence-led publication and decision toolkit for AI hardware, AI work and agents, and AI in pharmaceutical and wet-lab research. Publish in small, independently useful releases, beginning with a reusable editorial prompt and a high-intent local-AI hardware upgrade guide with visible reader FAQs.

The goal is to earn trust and qualified readership by answering consequential questions well. Search visibility, ad eligibility, affiliate conversion, and revenue are outcomes to measure; none is guaranteed by publication.

## Current product and content context

- The canonical editorial catalog is `lib/content/articles.json`; types and mapping live in `lib/editorial.ts`.
- Article pages in `app/[category]/[slug]/page.tsx` render a quick answer, sourced sections, tables/checklists, source dates, corrections path, and related reads. Article JSON-LD is generated by `components/JsonLd.tsx`.
- Content validation is in `scripts/verify-content.cjs`; `npm run check` and `npm run build` are the main repository checks.
- Current article sections support source references, tables, and checklists. The mapped article FAQ collection is currently empty, and there is no authored FAQ field in the article JSON schema. The first release must add visible FAQs through the existing content pipeline before claiming the articles have an FAQ feature.
- The workstation planner already takes market, budget, workload, and system path. The future component builder should extend this reader journey rather than replace the planner.
- The site has an existing desktop fine-pointer follower in `components/PointerAccent.tsx`; a pointer task should refine it, retain the native pointer, and preserve reduced-motion and touch behavior.
- The supplied Search Console screenshot is only an early baseline (154 impressions and 2 clicks in the selected three-month view). Do not claim it establishes demand for any specific new page.

## Product boundaries and release order

Treat these as separate releases with separate specs and acceptance checks. Do not combine them into a single large implementation.

1. **Editorial standard and hardware upgrade guide (first):** Create a durable research-and-writing prompt, extend article data/rendering to support a visible FAQ, and publish one original guide that helps readers decide whether to upgrade a local-AI PC, build a desktop, or choose a laptop/workstation. Link to the existing planner. Use sourced, dated country-specific examples only where verified.
2. **PC component builder and comparison:** Let readers choose compatible components, compare an estimated desktop build with laptops and prebuilt workstations, and see workload fit, constraints, upgradeability, and sourced prices. Keep estimation separate from measured performance and include market/date/tax/shipping caveats. No accounts, user submissions, or price API until separately designed and justified.
3. **AI hardware and price watch:** Cover CPU, GPU, NPU, TPU, RAM, storage, and relevant platforms from NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, Apple, and Chinese vendors when evidence is available. Each item has a source, checked date, market/currency, status (confirmed, reported, rumor, or estimate), and clear spec/price provenance. Rumors never appear as confirmed facts or as recommendations.
4. **AI and work:** Report task-level evidence, adoption data, affected workflows, uncertainty, and worker implications. Separate observed changes from forecasts; avoid universal job-loss claims.
5. **Agent systems, swarms, and failures:** Explain agent architectures and multi-agent coordination, then examine reliability and alleged “rogue” behavior. Distinguish lab demonstrations, benchmark results, operational incidents, and unverified anecdotes. Define the threat model and human/tool permissions; do not sensationalize.
6. **AI in pharma and wet labs:** Cover computational discovery, experiment design, robotic automation, and experimental validation as distinct stages. State whether each result is simulated, computational, preclinical, lab-validated, or clinically validated. Do not turn early research into treatment advice or clinical efficacy claims.
7. **Pointer refinement:** Review and improve the existing cursor follower as a small independent UI change. Keep the browser’s native cursor, restrict it to fine pointers, honor `prefers-reduced-motion`, and make no pointer effect essential to interaction.

The editorial prompt applies across releases, but every new tool, article series, or UI change gets a narrow design/implementation plan and verification before production publication.

## Reusable editorial prompt

Use the following as the standing instruction for an editor/research agent. Fill in the assignment fields for each article; if a field cannot be verified, state that plainly rather than guessing.

> **Role and standard**
>
> Act as a Senior Technology Editor and Investigative Science Journalist, working with a Senior Hardware Analyst when the assignment covers components, system compatibility, benchmarks, or prices. Write for technically curious readers in the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, and Europe. Be rigorous, readable, independent, and specific. Do not claim personal experience, tests, credentials, access, or interviews that did not happen.
>
> **Assignment fields**
>
> - Topic and one reader decision/question: `[fill in]`
> - Article type: `[news / explainer / buying guide / research analysis / comparison]`
> - Intended reader and workload: `[fill in]`
> - Relevant markets and currency: `[fill in; use only markets with verified data]`
> - Target publication date and source-check date: `[fill in]`
> - FyreLinkz pages/tools to link where genuinely useful: `[fill in]`
>
> **Research before drafting**
>
> 1. Browse current primary sources before writing. Prefer original research papers, datasets, model cards, official technical documentation, regulatory or institutional records, manufacturer specifications, product changelogs, and named regional retailers. Use reputable independent reporting for context and corroboration. A press release establishes what an organization claims or announces; it does not independently establish performance or impact.
> 2. Open and inspect the sources. Record publisher, exact title, URL, publication/update date, access/check date, and which claims each source supports. Use at least two independent sources for consequential, contested, or safety-relevant claims when available; explain if only one credible primary source exists.
> 3. Match evidence to each claim. Do not combine benchmark scores from different tasks, hardware, software, prompts, datasets, quantization, or measurement methods as if directly comparable. Attribute vendor, paper-author, and independent results precisely. Preserve test conditions and sample limits.
> 4. Separate confirmed facts, attributed claims, estimates, forecasts, rumors, and editorial inference. Date fast-changing specifications, prices, availability, model versions, policies, and licenses. Never present an undated price as current. If a fact cannot be verified, omit it or label the uncertainty and its consequence.
> 5. For AI-and-work stories, analyze tasks and workflows before occupations; distinguish measured adoption/productivity or employment data from projections and anecdotes. Include timeframe, population, methods, counterevidence, and uncertainty.
> 6. For agents and “rogue” claims, distinguish controlled demonstrations, evaluations, documented production incidents, and unverified accounts. Explain what tools/permissions were available, what actually happened, whether harm occurred, and what human controls existed. Do not use “rogue” as a factual label unless sources establish behavior that warrants it; otherwise attribute it as a claim or use a precise description.
> 7. For pharma, medicine, biology, or wet-lab claims, identify the evidence stage: computational, simulated, retrospective, preclinical, experimentally validated, clinical, or regulatory. Do not imply patient benefit, safety, treatment efficacy, or readiness beyond the evidence. No individualized medical advice. Explain reproducibility, sample size, controls, validation, and biosafety context where relevant.
> 8. For hardware, verify component compatibility and specifications against manufacturers or primary documentation. Prices need a named retailer/product, market, currency, date checked, and tax/shipping caveat. Distinguish estimated fit from a tested build. Identify the exact workload and memory constraints; never guarantee a model will run from parameter count alone.
>
> **Write an original, useful article**
>
> Answer one real reader question. Explain what changed or what choice the reader faces, who the information helps, trade-offs, failure cases, cost/availability limits, and a practical next step. Add original synthesis, calculations, a decision table, reproducible checklist, or clearly labeled analysis that the sources do not already provide as a packaged conclusion. Do not copy, closely paraphrase, or imitate another publication’s structure, phrasing, or distinctive presentation. Do not invent quotations, numbers, case studies, images, tests, or source access.
>
> Use this article structure when suitable: (1) descriptive title; (2) concise search description; (3) direct answer/quick takeaway near the top; (4) dated context and method; (5) evidence-led sections with citations near the claims; (6) useful comparison or checklist; (7) limitations and what remains unknown; (8) visible FAQs based on genuine follow-up questions; (9) sources and correction/update note. Change the structure when the reader’s task is better served by another form; do not pad to a target word count.
>
> **Search and AEO**
>
> Use natural language matching the reader’s intent, including specific product/workload/market terms only when the article substantively answers them. Never keyword-stuff, create near-duplicate country pages, promise rankings, or claim a guaranteed featured snippet/FAQ rich result. Use clear headings, concise definitions, a direct answer that stands on its own, and FAQs that add genuinely useful detail. Metadata must accurately summarize the article; structured data must match visible content and the site’s current schema.
>
> **Commercial independence and final audit**
>
> Disclose affiliate, sponsor, or other material relationships where applicable. Recommendations must follow stated editorial criteria, not commission. Add internal links only when they help the reader. Before delivery, run a sentence-level fact check against the source ledger; verify dates, names, units, math, licenses, prices, URLs, and internal routes; mark every unsupported claim for removal or qualification. Confirm image provenance/rights and accurate alt text. Deliver: final article in the repository’s article format, page title/description, FAQ questions and answers, source ledger with claim mapping and check dates, an original diagram/thumbnail brief, internal-link suggestions, and a short fact-check report listing unresolved uncertainty. Do not publish if a material claim remains unsupported.

## First article design

Working title: **“Upgrade your PC for local AI or build new? A GPU, RAM and laptop decision guide”**. The title is provisional until current Search Console query/page exports and fresh source research are checked.

The guide will help readers choose among upgrading an existing desktop, building a new one, buying a laptop, or selecting a workstation. It will explain GPU memory, system RAM, storage, CPU/platform, power/cooling, portability, and upgradeability in relation to local image generation, local LLM inference, and mixed creative-AI workloads. It will connect to the existing workstation planner and, when available, the component comparison tool.

It must include an explicit methodology and last-checked date; distinguish sourced specification guidance from FyreLinkz testing; show US/UK/Canada and selected European examples only where current sources support them; state local currency, VAT/sales-tax, shipping, and stock limits; and avoid implying that EU pricing is uniform. It should include a short decision table, a pre-purchase compatibility checklist, and visible FAQs. Any price/parts list has an owner and refresh interval; stale prices cannot support “best value” claims.

## First-release article and FAQ data design

- Add typed FAQ question/answer data to the existing editorial input model and JSON content format.
- Render FAQ content visibly on the article page with semantic headings and keyboard-accessible disclosure behavior only if collapsed UI keeps answers discoverable and accessible; otherwise render short open answers.
- Ensure JSON-LD exactly reflects visible FAQ content and valid article content. Do not imply Google will show FAQ rich results.
- Extend content verification for non-empty question/answer, duplicate question detection, reasonable answer length, and no unsupported claims in schema output. Preserve existing slug, image, citations, source-check date, and date checks.
- Do not make all existing articles require FAQ data in the first release; add FAQs to the new guide and migrate other articles only as separately reviewed.

## Story and data quality controls

- Keep content editorially independent and source-first. If the subject is materially disputed, include the strongest credible counterevidence and explain its limits.
- The source ledger links each consequential factual claim to the evidence used; citation adjacency in the rendered article must be clear.
- Every article has a human-readable update policy. Update `updatedAt` only for substantive changes, and refresh `sourceCheckedAt` only after rechecking the sources.
- Do not auto-publish trend content. A human editor must approve claims, final copy, images, disclosures, and date-sensitive facts.
- Treat traffic, search impressions, affiliate income, and ad eligibility as measured outcomes; no prompt may forecast a guaranteed level of any of them.

## Acceptance criteria for the first release

1. The new article answers one high-intent local-AI purchasing question with useful original decision support and no keyword stuffing.
2. Every changing price/spec/availability claim has a source and check date, or is explicitly omitted/qualified.
3. Vendor and paper claims are attributed; no unperformed FyreLinkz test is implied.
4. Visible FAQs are useful, source-consistent, and represented accurately in structured data.
5. Reader can follow citations, use the existing planner, understand market/currency assumptions, and find the correction path.
6. Existing articles, route generation, sitemap/RSS inclusion, dark/light rendering, mobile layout, and article metadata continue to work.
7. Required validation: content checks, TypeScript, production build, route/metadata and JSON-LD checks, and browser verification for desktop/mobile, keyboard access, and visible FAQ behavior.
8. Pointer work is reviewed as its own small task and must remain absent on touch/coarse pointers and reduced-motion mode.

## Risks and mitigations

- **Too many topics at once:** stage the work; each phase has its own spec and acceptance criteria.
- **Stale price/model news:** show checked dates and status labels; suppress price-led recommendations when stale.
- **Hype in agent/biomedical stories:** require evidence-stage and claim labels, primary sources, and independent corroboration where possible.
- **Search over-optimization:** answer reader tasks first; avoid thin location variants and repeated keyword phrases.
- **False promise of FAQ search features:** treat FAQs as reader UX and structured data hygiene; make no rich-result promise.
- **Health/safety harm:** report research scope and validation limits; no personalized medical advice, unsafe lab protocols, or unsupported clinical claims.

## Deferred decisions

- Final first-article title and exact query targets depend on a fresh Search Console export and source review.
- Builder catalog size, exact retailer integrations, live-price feeds, component compatibility depth, and monetization depend on separate product specs.
- The hardware tracker’s update frequency and source policy need a separate data-operations design.
- Exact order of the AI jobs, agents, and wet-lab story packages may be adjusted based on source quality and reader evidence, while preserving the staged boundaries above.
