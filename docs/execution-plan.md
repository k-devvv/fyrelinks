# Execution plan: getting to real income, not pennies

Written 31 October 2026. Target used throughout: **₹1,00,000 per month** (roughly $1,200).
Every figure is arithmetic from a stated assumption, not a forecast. Commission rates and
platform terms change and must be checked against the actual programme before relying on
any line here.

## The arithmetic that decides everything

How many customers does ₹1L a month need, by route?

| Route | Revenue per customer | Customers per month | Traffic needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display ads at $2 RPM (consumer-tech rate) | ~₹0.17 per visit | — | **~600,000 visits** |
| Amazon affiliate, 1–2% on a ₹2L build | ₹2,000–4,000 per sale | 25–50 sales | ~10,000–50,000 qualified visits |
| Digital product at ₹1,500 | ₹1,500 | **67 sales** | large audience or paid traffic |
| Local business retainer | ₹4,000/month | **25 clients** | none; outreach |
| Project work | ₹25,000 | **4 projects** | none; outreach |
| One Western retainer billed in USD | $1,200/month | **1 client** | none; outreach |

The site currently gets roughly 154 impressions a quarter. Read the right-hand column
again: every traffic-funded route needs volume that is three to four orders of magnitude
away. Every customer-funded route needs between one and twenty-five people to say yes.

## The root cause of "pennies"

It is not the monetisation method, the hosting, or the traffic. It is **who the customer
is**. FyreLinkz serves ComfyUI users, AI video hobbyists and people building their own PC.
That is a consumer audience, and consumer audiences monetise at a fraction of a rupee per
visit unless the scale is enormous. Photopea only escapes this with 12M monthly users.

Businesses pay 100–1000x more per customer for the same hour of work, because the spend is
a cost against their revenue rather than discretionary. The fastest change available is not
a better funnel; it is a different buyer.

## Strategy

**FyreLinkz stops being the business and becomes the credential.** A published, sourced,
technically specific site on AI video and local AI is worth more as proof of expertise in a
sales conversation than as an ad-supported property. Keep publishing, keep the affiliate
links, expect them to earn little, and stop measuring the project by them.

**The business is selling AI production or automation to businesses, priced in USD where
possible.** An Indian cost base against Western pricing is the single largest lever here:
₹1L a month is one modest Western retainer, not six hundred thousand pageviews.

## Phase 1 — weeks 1 to 4: find out if anyone pays

Target: **two paying clients at ₹25,000 or more.** No new code.

1. Pick exactly one offer and one buyer. Candidates, in order of fit with existing skills:
   - AI video production for D2C brands and marketing agencies (the model costs, workflows
     and quality limits are already documented on the site).
   - AI workflow automation for small businesses: document extraction, lead enrichment,
     reporting.
   - Technical AI engineering help for teams already shipping AI features.
2. Build a list of 50 named prospects. Agencies first: they have clients and no capacity,
   so one relationship becomes recurring work with no marketing.
3. Send 50 individual messages. Lead with a specific observation about their work, not a
   service description. Ask one question.
4. Take every call. The goal of Phase 1 is not revenue, it is learning what people will
   pay for and what they say no to.

Phase 1 fails if fewer than 10 of 50 reply. That is a signal about the offer or the list,
not about the market, and the correct response is to change the offer and send 50 more.

## Phase 2 — weeks 5 to 10: deliver and raise the price

1. Deliver the first two projects properly. Over-deliver on the first one.
2. Write each up as a case study with real numbers, published on FyreLinkz. This is the
   first time the site earns its keep.
3. Raise the price for the third client. Keep raising it until prospects hesitate.
4. Ask every delivered client for one referral.

## Phase 3 — weeks 11 onward: productise the repeated part

Only once income exists:

1. Identify what was rebuilt in every project. That, and only that, becomes a product.
2. Convert it to a fixed-price SKU on the site with a Gumroad button.
3. Build the client-side tools from `revenue-ideas.md` as funnel entry points for that SKU,
   not as an advertising business. At a few thousand visits a month their ad value is a few
   dollars; their value as qualified leads for a ₹25,000 service is not.
4. Add email capture at the same time; it is what makes the tools compound.

## Phase 4 — later: assets and exit

Paid backend spend becomes justifiable here, not before. Content and affiliate properties
sell at 20–50x monthly profit, and buyers pay a premium for diversified revenue, an owned
audience and documented systems — which is why the email list and the case studies are
worth more than their direct income suggests.

## What has to be accepted

There is no version of solo, fast, meaningful income that avoids talking to buyers. Every
route that avoids sales — ads, affiliate at scale, products to strangers, a tool suite —
requires an audience that takes months or years to build and may never arrive. The services
route trades scalability for a short path to cash, and the honest costs are that it does not
scale, income is lumpy, and it consumes the hours that would otherwise build the assets.

The recommended sequence funds the assets with the services rather than choosing between
them.

## Not doing

- Chasing display-ad revenue at consumer-tech RPM.
- Building more tools before one person has paid for anything.
- Any paid infrastructure until income covers it (see the free-tier constraint).
- Promising anyone, including ourselves, a timeline for search traffic.
